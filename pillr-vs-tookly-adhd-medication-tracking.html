Pillr vs Tookly
for ADHD medication tracking

Pillr and Tookly are both Apple-focused medication trackers with reminders, dose history, as-needed records, refill tools, and privacy-first positioning. Pillr adds ADHD focus-window tracking, structured reflections, exports, and appointment notes, while Tookly puts more emphasis on reminder escalation, widgets, streaks, and a missed-dose checklist.

Published September 4, 2026 · Last reviewed September 4, 2026

A note from Pillr

We make Pillr, so this is not an independent review. This page explains how we compare Tookly with Pillr for ADHD medication reminders, dose history, refills, privacy, and everyday follow-through.

Quick answer

Choose Pillr if you want reminders and clear dose history beside estimated focus windows, structured focus, mood, symptom, and side-effect reflections, refill records, CSV or PDF exports, and appointment notes.

Choose Tookly if the reminder itself needs more escalation and you want a choice of calm, repeating, or supported full-screen alarm styles, plus an interactive widget, Live Activity, streaks, a monthly history view, and optional iCloud sync.

Both can be downloaded free and both reserve some advanced features for paid access. Neither is available on Android. The practical choice is whether you need Pillr's fuller ADHD medication record or Tookly's stronger emphasis on reminder presentation and Apple system surfaces.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone choosing between two medication trackers built around iPhone reminders and fast dose records. It is especially useful if the decision turns on reminder persistence, explicit missed-dose review, ADHD focus windows, reflections, widgets, refill detail, exports, appointment preparation, or optional iCloud sync.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, setting an interval, deciding what to do after a missed dose, or treating an app's timing view as medical advice.

Key differences

Pillr is ADHD medication-record led. It connects reminders and a chronological dose history with an estimated Focus Timeline, structured reflections, refill periods, CSV or PDF exports, and appointment preparation.

Tookly is reminder-escalation led. Its current App Store page lists Gentle and Nagging reminder modes, an optional Strong Alarm on supported iPhones, interactive widgets, a reminder Live Activity, streaks, a monthly history view, and a missed-dose catch-up checklist.

The overlap is real. Both support medication reminders, dose history, as-needed medication, pill supply, refill alerts, Apple widgets, no product account, and local-first medication storage. The clearest decision is whether you want a deeper ADHD medication review record or more ways for the reminder to get your attention.

Detailed comparison by use case

If reminder persistence is the main problem

Tookly offers three reminder styles. Its Nagging mode repeats until the user responds within a chosen limit, while its optional Strong Alarm uses Apple's alarm permission on supported iPhones. Tookly also lists a Focus Filter for controlling reminders around iPhone Focus modes.

Pillr provides scheduled medication alerts with Take now and Remind in 15 mins actions, plus in-app snoozing and optional Adaptive Reminders for the remaining schedule. Test either app with your notification, Focus, sound, and device settings before relying on it. No app can guarantee every operating-system alert will arrive exactly on time.

If reviewing missed or unhandled doses matters

Pillr keeps scheduled doses on a daily timeline as upcoming, overdue, taken, or skipped. A dose remains visibly unhandled until it is logged, and history can be corrected through the app's Undo and manual-entry tools.

Tookly's current version history describes a Catch Up checklist for reviewing missed doses one at a time, plus a calendar and heatmap for dose history. It also lets a mistaken log be deleted with a short Undo period.

If ADHD focus windows and reflections matter

Pillr has a dedicated estimated Focus Timeline based on timing saved for the medication, along with structured focus, mood, symptom, and side-effect reflections. These are records for organising what you noticed, not proof of how medication worked.

Tookly's current public information focuses on reminders, dose states, streaks, supply, widgets, and reports rather than a focus-window or reflection workflow. If those records are central to your medication review, Pillr is the clearer fit.

If refills, exports, and appointments matter

Both apps track remaining supply and refill alerts. Pillr keeps each refill or stock correction in a separate inventory period and supports CSV or PDF medication-history exports. My Appointment adds questions, local recordings, editable summaries, and follow-up notes.

Tookly lists an estimated run-out date, low-stock alerts, CSV export, and a 35-day adherence report with its paid plan. Open a sample export before relying on either app for a clinician appointment.

If privacy, accounts, and backup decide the choice

Pillr does not have a Pillr account or medication cloud-sync system. Medication records and checked recovery copies stay on the device. The Pillr privacy page explains medication lookup, optional AI features, Apple Health access, and fixed product-interaction analytics separately.

Tookly does not require an account. Its current privacy policy says medication data is stored locally and may sync through Apple iCloud when the user is signed in. It separately describes Firebase Analytics, Crashlytics, and subscription processing, while saying medication names, doses, and health records are not sent to Firebase.

Where Pillr fits better

  • You want estimated focus windows and structured reflections beside dose history.
  • You want separate refill periods, CSV or PDF history exports, and appointment notes.
  • You prefer on-device medication records without a medication cloud-sync option.
  • You want the medication record itself to lead the daily experience.

Where Tookly fits better

  • You want a choice between calm, repeating, and supported full-screen reminder styles.
  • You want an interactive widget and reminder Live Activity to lead the experience.
  • You want a missed-dose catch-up checklist, monthly heatmap, and streak view.
  • You want optional Apple iCloud syncing without creating a Tookly account.

Why Pillr is a strong fit for ADHD medication tracking

Pillr is strongest when the goal is a fuller ADHD medication record: what was due, what was taken or skipped, what remains unhandled, the estimated focus window, what you noticed afterward, how much supply remains, and what you want to bring to an appointment.

Pillr does not tell you what medication to take, when to take it, or how to respond to a side effect or missed dose. It keeps factual records and reminders together so you can review them and discuss questions with a qualified healthcare professional.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app supports your exact medication and prescribed schedule.
  • How taken, skipped, missed, late, snoozed, and as-needed doses appear in history.
  • Whether the reminder style works with your iPhone's notification, Focus, sound, and alarm settings.
  • Which reminder, report, widget, reflection, refill, and appointment features need paid access.
  • Whether you want medication records kept only on the device or optionally synced through iCloud.
  • Whether the export format contains the dates, actions, notes, and period you need.
  • Whether every phone or tablet you plan to use is currently supported.

  • Use Tookly for stronger reminder escalation, an interactive widget, and missed-dose catch-up.
  • Choose the storage and backup model that fits how you want medication data handled.

No universal winner here. Choose the reminder, record, and privacy setup you are most likely to keep using.

Is Pillr or Tookly better for ADHD medication tracking?

Pillr may fit better if you want focus-window estimates, structured reflections, refill records, CSV or PDF history exports, and appointment notes. Tookly may fit better if reminder escalation, an interactive widget, a missed-dose checklist, and optional iCloud sync lead the decision.

Do Pillr and Tookly require an account?

No. Neither app currently requires a product account. Pillr keeps medication records and checked recovery copies on the device without medication cloud sync. Tookly describes local storage with optional Apple iCloud syncing.

Do Pillr and Tookly support as-needed medication?

Yes. Pillr keeps as-needed medication in its Cabinet without scheduled reminders. Tookly lists as-needed logging with user-configured interval and weekly-cap controls. Those settings must follow instructions from a clinician or pharmacist.

Are Pillr and Tookly available on Android?

No. Both are currently Apple-platform apps. Pillr's listing includes iPhone and iPad, while Tookly is currently listed for iPhone only.
