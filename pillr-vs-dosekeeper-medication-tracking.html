Pillr vs DoseKeeper
for medication tracking

Pillr and DoseKeeper both organise medication reminders, dose records, supply, notes, and reports on Apple devices. Pillr centres ADHD medication follow-through, focus windows, reflections, and appointment preparation. DoseKeeper centres broader scheduling, grouped reminders, blister packs, batch inventory, expiry dates, a health diary, and Apple Watch tools.

Published September 25, 2026 · Last reviewed September 25, 2026

A note from Pillr

We make Pillr, so this is not an independent review. This page explains how we compare DoseKeeper with Pillr for medication reminders, dose history, ADHD tracking, supply, diary notes, reports, and privacy.

Quick answer

Choose Pillr if you want an ADHD-focused medication record with scheduled and as-needed medication, clear taken and skipped history, estimated focus windows, medication-benefit and side-effect-impact ratings, refill periods, CSV/PDF exports, and appointment notes.

Choose DoseKeeper if grouped reminders, supported alarm options, blister and batch inventory, expiry alerts, Apple Watch logging, NFC medicine tags, a wider health diary, or detailed PDF reports matter more.

Both are Apple-platform apps with free limits and paid advanced features. DoseKeeper currently requires recent Apple system software, so device compatibility may decide the choice before features do.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone choosing between an ADHD-focused medication record and a broader Apple-platform medication manager. It is especially useful when focus-window tracking, as-needed medication, blister packs, grouped reminders, diary notes, Apple Watch logging, reports, privacy, or device requirements will decide the fit.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, setting medical timing, interpreting an adherence percentage, or deciding what to do after a missed dose.

Key differences

Pillr is ADHD-medication-record led. It connects reminders and a chronological dose history with an estimated Focus Timeline, simple reflections, refill periods, CSV/PDF exports, and appointment preparation.

DoseKeeper is broader medication-management led. Its current product site and App Store page describe grouped reminders, blister packs, batch inventory, expiry alerts, a health diary, Apple Watch logging, NFC tags, and several PDF report types.

The overlap is strongest around reminders, dose history, supply, notes, and reports. The practical choice is whether you want a focused ADHD timeline and appointment record or a wider medication organiser with more Apple-device and inventory tools.

Detailed comparison by use case

If clear ADHD dose history is the main need

Pillr explicitly separates scheduled, overdue, taken, skipped, snoozed, and as-needed medication. It keeps the planned time separate from the actual logged time and supports corrections through Undo and manual history tools.

DoseKeeper records scheduled medication as taken, missed, or pending and can record medicines scheduled together from one reminder. Its current public information does not clearly document every skipped, postponed, corrected, or as-needed state, so verify the states your routine needs before moving an established record.

If focus windows, symptoms, or diary context matter

Pillr estimates a stimulant focus window from timing saved by the user and the dose they log. Reflections keep separate medication-benefit and side-effect-impact ratings with optional notes. These records organise observations; they do not measure medication effectiveness.

DoseKeeper has a wider health diary for free-form entries, symptoms, side effects, moods, and medicine links. On supported devices, Apple Intelligence can create short on-device summaries and tags. Those patterns and summaries remain informational, not a diagnosis or proof of cause.

If blister packs, supply, or expiry dates matter

Pillr tracks total pills, remaining supply, pills used per dose, low-stock thresholds, and separate refill periods. A new refill starts a new inventory period so older dose changes cannot alter the new supply.

DoseKeeper's inventory tools organise stock by batch, track expiry dates, show blister progress, estimate remaining units, and forecast refills. Some medicine forms are not reduced automatically, so check whether your medication can be represented accurately.

If reminders and quick access matter

Pillr supports scheduled reminders, snooze, follow-ups, and optional adaptive timing inside its medication timeline. Cabinet medications are kept separate as as-needed records without scheduled reminders.

DoseKeeper supports different days and times, interval planning, missed-dose follow-ups, grouped reminders, Apple Watch logging, App Shortcuts, and NFC medicine tags. Its reminder guide distinguishes standard notifications from alarm-based and critical-alert options, which are limited to supported schedules and device configurations.

If reports and appointment preparation matter

Pillr exports medication history as CSV or PDF and keeps My Appointment questions, recordings, transcripts, editable summaries, and follow-up notes locally on supported devices.

DoseKeeper can create PDF reports for medication logs, inventory, trends, scheduled-dose insights, diary entries, and selected blood-pressure workflows. Check whether the report includes the exact dates, people, medicines, and context your appointment needs.

If privacy decides the choice

Pillr has no Pillr account or medication cloud-sync system. Medication records, reflections, appointment content, and checked recovery copies stay on the device. The Pillr privacy page explains optional medication lookup, analytics, Apple Health access, and other services separately.

DoseKeeper's privacy page says it does not collect personal information in the app or on its website, uses no advertising, and performs no behaviour tracking. Apple's current App Store label says Data Not Collected. DoseKeeper version 4.4 also says its optional Deep Scanning can use Apple's Private Cloud Compute, so review Apple's current service requirements if you plan to scan packaging.

Where Pillr fits better

  • You want clear taken, skipped, overdue, snoozed, corrected, and as-needed dose states.
  • You want estimated focus windows and structured reflections beside ADHD medication history.
  • You want refill periods, CSV/PDF history exports, and appointment preparation in one focused app.
  • You want medication records and checked recovery copies to stay on the device without a product account.

Where DoseKeeper fits better

  • You want blister, batch, expiry, and refill-forecast tools.
  • You want a wider symptom, side-effect, mood, and free-form diary.
  • You want grouped reminders, Apple Watch logging, NFC tags, or a broader set of PDF reports.
  • Your devices meet DoseKeeper's current Apple system requirements.

Why Pillr is a strong fit for ADHD medication tracking

Pillr is strongest when the daily ADHD medication record is the main job: what was due, what was taken or skipped, what remains unhandled, the estimated focus window, what you noticed afterward, how much supply remains, and what you want to bring to an appointment.

That narrower focus keeps reminders, history, reflections, refills, and appointment preparation together without requiring a product account or turning the record into a medical recommendation. Pillr does not tell you what medication to take or how to respond to a missed dose or side effect.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app supports your exact prescribed schedule and as-needed workflow.
  • How taken, skipped, missed, late, corrected, snoozed, and unrecorded doses appear.
  • Whether your Apple devices meet the app's current operating-system requirements.
  • Which reminder, inventory, diary, report, and history features need paid access.
  • How fractional doses, liquids, patches, injections, or other medicine forms affect stock totals.
  • Whether grouped reminders and alarm-based alerts work with your schedule and permissions.
  • Whether a report contains the dates, medicines, notes, and period your appointment needs.
  • What optional Apple or third-party services are used by the features you plan to enable.

Is Pillr or DoseKeeper better for medication tracking?

Pillr may fit better if you want an ADHD-focused record with clear dose states, estimated focus windows, structured reflections, refill periods, exports, and appointment notes. DoseKeeper may fit better if grouped reminders, blister and batch tracking, expiry alerts, a wider health diary, Apple Watch logging, NFC access, or detailed PDF reports matter more.

Can Pillr and DoseKeeper be used for free?

Yes, with limits. Pillr supports up to three active medications for free, while DoseKeeper says its free tier supports up to three medicines and two scheduled doses. Each app reserves some advanced features or higher limits for paid access, so check the current App Store offer.

How do Pillr and DoseKeeper handle privacy?

Pillr has no Pillr account and keeps its medication record and checked recovery copies on the device. DoseKeeper says it does not collect app data, has no advertising or tracking, and currently carries Apple's Data Not Collected label. Review each app's current privacy information for optional services before choosing.

Are Pillr and DoseKeeper available on Android?

No. Both are currently Apple-platform apps. Pillr is available on iPhone and iPad. DoseKeeper lists iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, Mac, and Apple Vision compatibility, with recent system requirements that should be checked before downloading.
