Pillr vs Doz
for medication tracking

Pillr and Doz both offer private medication reminders, taken or skipped history, as-needed records, and refill tracking. Pillr centres ADHD medication follow-through and appointment preparation, while Doz organises medication around prescriptions, meals, and flexible iPhone reminders.

Published August 10, 2026 · Last reviewed August 21, 2026

A note from Pillr

We make Pillr, so this is not an independent review. This page explains how we compare Doz with Pillr for ADHD medication tracking and prescription-based medication routines.

Quick answer

Choose Pillr if ADHD medication is the main routine you want to track and you want reminders, quick taken or skipped history, estimated focus windows, reflections, refill planning, exports, and appointment notes in one local-first app.

Choose Doz if you want an iPhone medication tracker that groups medicines by prescription and gives meal-linked schedules, Critical Alerts, follow-up nudges, interactive widgets, detailed prescription records, quick as-needed history, stock tracking, and optional private iCloud syncing.

Both can be downloaded free and both list paid options. The practical choice is whether you need an ADHD-focused record around each dose or a prescription-first schedule built around everyday routines.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone choosing between two private Apple medication trackers, especially when ADHD follow-through, prescription grouping, meal-based reminders, dose history, refill planning, appointment notes, or iCloud syncing will decide the fit.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, setting medical timing, or deciding what to do after a missed dose.

Key differences

Pillr is ADHD-routine led. It is built around quick taken or skipped records, an estimated Focus Timeline, structured reflections, pill counts, history exports, and appointment preparation.

Doz is prescription-routine led. Its current official site and App Store page describe prescription grouping, meal-linked timing, follow-up nudges, Critical Alerts, flexible schedules, widgets, detailed prescription records, dose history, and stock tracking.

The overlap is meaningful. Both support scheduled and as-needed medication, taken or skipped records, refill planning, local storage, and no required account. The clearest difference is what surrounds the dose record.

Detailed comparison by use case

If ADHD medication follow-through is the main job

Pillr is the clearer fit when the reminder is only one part of the record. The dose action, estimated focus window, reflection, pill supply, history, and appointment notes can be reviewed together.

This can help when you need to check what happened rather than rely on memory. For a wider guide to the decision, see what to look for in an ADHD medication reminder app.

If prescriptions and meal timing organise your routine

Doz groups medicines under the prescription they belong to. Its reminder setup covers before, during, or after meals, specific days, intervals, dayparts, and intake-and-pause cycles. It also describes follow-up nudges and an optional Critical Alert that can sound when the phone is muted or a Focus is active.

Its current iPhone release also adds prescriber, diagnosis, prescription dates, notes, completed-course status, and treatment filters. That can suit someone who wants the prescription itself to be the main organising record.

Those controls should still follow the schedule provided by your clinician. An app can organise a prescribed routine, but it should not create medication instructions.

If dose history or refills matter

Both apps record taken or skipped doses and keep medication supply near the daily routine. Pillr supports notes, actual logged times, decimal pill use, low-stock thresholds, refill reminders, and CSV or PDF history export.

Doz lists full history, calendar indicators, editable logged dates and times, quick as-needed logging with recent history, stock levels, low-stock thresholds, and one-tap refill actions. If supply planning is central, compare the current plan limits and see Pillr's refill and pill-count guide.

If you want a record for appointments

Pillr combines medication history with My Appointment preparation, local recording and transcription, an editable summary, and PDF export. It is the stronger fit when the app also needs to hold questions and follow-up notes for a medication review.

Doz offers medication history and progress insights overall, by medication, and by prescription. That may be enough for personal review, but check the current sharing or export options before relying on it for an appointment.

If privacy and account setup matter

Pillr has no Pillr account. Medication records and checked recovery copies stay on the iPhone; you can choose a checked copy to restore in Settings. Pillr does not offer cloud backup or syncing. The Pillr privacy page explains optional analytics and online features separately.

Doz's current privacy policy says medication names, prescriptions, and dosage schedules stay on the device. If iCloud sync is enabled, it uses the user's private Apple CloudKit database and says the developer cannot access the synced data. The policy separately describes anonymous app-use and diagnostic data and subscription handling without medication data.

Review current backup, sync, analytics, deletion, and payment details before entering a full medication history.

  • You want CSV or PDF history exports and a dedicated appointment record.
  • You want iPad support as well as iPhone.

Where Doz fits better

  • You want medicines grouped under the prescription they came from.
  • You need reminders linked to meals, intervals, dayparts, or cycles.
  • You want detailed prescription records and quick recent as-needed history.
  • You want optional Critical Alerts and interactive iPhone widgets.
  • You want private iCloud syncing across your Apple devices.

Why Pillr is a strong fit for ADHD medication tracking

Pillr is strongest when ADHD medication follow-through is the problem to solve. It keeps the reminder, completed action, focus-window estimate, reflection, refill status, medication history, and appointment preparation close together without requiring a Pillr account.

It does not tell you what medication to take, what dose to take, or how to respond to missed doses or side effects. It helps you organise a clearer record for your own review and for conversations with a qualified healthcare professional.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app supports your exact prescribed schedule and as-needed workflow.
  • How taken, skipped, late, edited, and unrecorded doses appear in history.
  • Which reminder, medication, refill, widget, history, and export features need a paid option.
  • Whether you need iPad support or iPhone-only use is enough.
  • Whether an appointment record, progress view, or shareable export matches your needs.
  • How local storage, syncing, analytics, deletion, and subscription handling work.

Is Pillr or Doz better for medication tracking?

Pillr may fit better if ADHD medication follow-through, focus-window estimates, reflections, appointment notes, and history exports matter most. Doz may fit better if you want prescription-grouped medication setup, meal-linked schedules, Critical Alerts, detailed prescription records, and private iCloud syncing on iPhone.

Do Pillr and Doz support as-needed medication?

Yes. Pillr keeps as-needed medication in its Cabinet flow, while Doz lists quick as-needed logging with quantities and a recent as-needed history. Check that either workflow matches the instructions from your clinician.

Do Pillr and Doz require an account?

No account is required for either app's core medication record. Pillr stores medication records and checked recovery copies on the iPhone, with a user-chosen restore in Settings; it does not offer cloud backup or syncing. Doz stores medication data locally and offers optional private iCloud sync through Apple's CloudKit.

Do Pillr or Doz replace medical advice?

No. They are reminder and record-keeping tools. Questions about doses, timing, side effects, interactions, and missed medication need advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
