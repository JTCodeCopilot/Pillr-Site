Pillr vs Dozly
for ADHD medication tracking

Pillr and Dozly both focus on ADHD-friendly medication reminders and lower-friction tracking. Pillr centres a clear local medication record with focus windows, reflections, refills, exports, and appointment notes. Dozly adds AI-assisted setup, symptom patterns, family alerts, refill predictions, and AI-generated reports.

A note from Pillr

We make Pillr, so this is not an independent review. This page explains how we compare Dozly with Pillr for ADHD medication reminders, dose history, symptoms, refills, privacy, and appointment preparation.

Quick answer

Choose Pillr if you want a local-first ADHD medication record with scheduled and as-needed doses, clear taken and skipped history, estimated focus windows, medication-benefit and side-effect-impact ratings, pill counts, refill alerts, exports, and appointment notes.

Choose Dozly if AI-assisted medication setup, persistent reminder styles, Low-Energy Mode, symptom tracking, family alerts, refill predictions, an AI interaction checker, and AI-generated appointment reports are more important to you.

The biggest boundary is data handling. Pillr's core medication record does not require an account or medication cloud sync. Dozly's privacy policy describes account information, server storage, analytics, subscriptions, and optional AI processing. Check the current sign-in and paid setup before choosing.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone choosing between two ADHD-focused medication trackers on Apple devices. It is especially useful when reminders, dose history, focus windows, symptoms, refills, family alerts, AI use, appointment reports, or privacy will decide the fit.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, setting medical timing, interpreting a symptom pattern as proof, or deciding what to do after a missed dose.

Key differences

Pillr is medication-record led. It connects reminders and a chronological dose history with an estimated Focus Timeline, simple reflections, refill periods, CSV/PDF exports, and appointment preparation.

Dozly is AI-assistance led. Its current website and App Store page describe AI-assisted setup, symptom analysis, an interaction checker, refill predictions, family alerts, Low-Energy Mode, and AI-generated reports.

The overlap is strongest around reminders, quick taken confirmation, symptom notes, refill planning, and appointment preparation. The clearest choice is whether you want a predictable local record or broader AI-supported and family-facing tools.

Detailed comparison by use case

If clear dose history is the main need

Pillr explicitly separates scheduled, overdue, taken, skipped, snoozed, and as-needed medication. It keeps the planned time separate from the actual logged time and supports corrections through Undo and manual history tools.

Dozly's public pages centre one-tap taken confirmation and medication history. They do not clearly explain how skipped, missed, late, corrected, or unrecorded doses appear, so check those states before moving an established record.

If focus windows, symptoms, or side effects matter

Pillr estimates a stimulant focus window from timing saved by the user and the dose they log. Reflections keep separate medication-benefit and side-effect-impact ratings with optional notes. These records organise observations; they do not measure medication effectiveness.

Dozly lists symptom tracking, AI-supported symptom analysis, and correlations between medication and symptoms. Treat those patterns as prompts for a conversation, not proof that a medicine caused an outcome. Pillr's side-effect tracking guide explains how to keep notes factual.

If refills, family alerts, or reports matter

Pillr tracks total pills, remaining supply, pills used per dose, low-stock thresholds, and separate refill periods. It can export medication history as CSV or PDF and keep a local My Appointment record with questions, recording, transcription, an editable summary, and follow-up notes.

Dozly lists refill predictions, family notifications, and AI-generated reports in paid access. Confirm what a family member can see, how the invitation can be removed, which details appear in a report, and whether the current plan includes the feature you need.

If AI medication tools matter

Dozly lists AI-assisted medication setup, symptom analysis, interaction checks, prescription scanning, weekly insights, and report generation. Its terms say interaction information may be incomplete and should not be relied on alone.

Dozly's privacy policy says these AI features require explicit consent and can send messages, medication names, dosages, frequencies, symptom data, and conversation context to Anthropic. Dozly says email, user ID, pharmacy details, prescription numbers, and full adherence history are excluded from that AI transfer.

Pillr's medication reminders, dose history, pill supply, Focus Timeline, reflections, and exports do not depend on online AI. Its label scanner reads the image on device, and My Appointment transcription and summary processing are local on supported devices.

If privacy and account setup decide the choice

Pillr has no Pillr account or medication cloud-sync system. Medication records, reflections, appointment content, and checked recovery copies stay on the device. The Pillr privacy page explains optional medication lookup, analytics, Apple Health access, and other services separately.

Dozly's website says no account is needed, while its privacy policy discusses account information supplied at sign-up, encrypted server storage, account deletion, subscriptions, analytics, crash reporting, push delivery, and AI processing. Confirm which setup applies to the current app and the features you plan to use.

Dozly's website and App Store also show different paid amounts, so this comparison does not publish an exact price. Check the current in-app offer and renewal terms before subscribing.

Where Dozly fits better

  • You want AI-assisted medication setup and symptom pattern tools.
  • You want Low-Energy Mode and persistent reminder framing designed around ADHD.
  • You want optional family alerts, refill predictions, or AI-generated reports.
  • You are comfortable reviewing the current account, server-storage, analytics, subscription, and AI-consent setup.

Why Pillr is a strong fit for ADHD medication tracking

Pillr is strongest when the daily medication record is the main job: what was due, what was taken or skipped, what remains unhandled, the estimated focus window, what you noticed afterward, how much supply remains, and what you want to bring to an appointment.

That narrower focus keeps reminders, history, reflections, refills, and appointment preparation together without requiring a product account or AI-generated interpretation. Pillr does not tell you what medication to take or how to respond to a missed dose or side effect.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app supports your exact prescribed schedule and as-needed workflow.
  • How taken, skipped, missed, late, corrected, snoozed, and unrecorded doses appear.
  • Which reminder, symptom, refill, family, report, and history features need paid access.
  • Whether a family alert shares the right information with the right person.
  • Whether a report contains the dates, actions, notes, and period your appointment needs.
  • Whether account creation is required for the Dozly features you plan to use.
  • What health information is stored on a server or sent to an AI provider, and how consent and deletion work.
  • The current in-app price and renewal terms in your App Store region.

Is Pillr or Dozly better for ADHD medication tracking?

Pillr may fit better if you want clear taken and skipped dose records, estimated focus windows, structured reflections, refill records, CSV or PDF history exports, and appointment notes in a local-first app. Dozly may fit better if AI-assisted setup, symptom patterns, family alerts, refill predictions, and AI-generated reports matter more.

Do Pillr and Dozly require an account?

Pillr does not require a Pillr account and keeps medication records and checked recovery copies on the device. Dozly's website says no account is needed, while its privacy policy discusses account information, server storage, and account deletion. Confirm the current sign-in setup before choosing.

Can Dozly's interaction checker replace a pharmacist or doctor?

No. Dozly's own terms say its interaction information may be incomplete and should not be relied on alone. Medication interactions, doses, timing, side effects, and missed doses should be discussed with a pharmacist, doctor, or other qualified healthcare professional.

Are Pillr and Dozly available on Android?

No. Both are currently Apple-platform apps. Their current App Store listings include iPhone and iPad, while Dozly's website says an Android version is still in development.
