Adult ADHD appointment guide

ADHD telehealth appointment
checklist for adults

A video appointment can move quickly. For adults with ADHD, the hardest part may be remembering what happened between visits.

A short prep list can help you bring clearer dose history, routine notes, and questions into the conversation.

Speak with your doctor, psychiatrist, pharmacist, or prescribing clinician about medication timing, missed doses, side effects, or treatment decisions.

Why telehealth prep matters

A 2024 CDC report found that approximately one half of surveyed U.S. adults with ADHD had ever used telehealth for ADHD-related services.

A video visit can still feel rushed, distracted, or squeezed between work and family. A short setup and a one-page record can help you spend less of the call rebuilding the last few weeks from memory.

Use a five-minute pre-call setup

Telehealth adds a few practical steps that an in-person appointment does not. Keep them small and visible.

When Quick check
10 minutes before Open the visit link, test audio and video, charge the device, and have headphones nearby if you use them.
5 minutes before Open your medication list, recent dose history, side-effect or symptom notes, and top questions.
During the visit Keep the question list visible. Ask permission before recording anyone and check any transcript or summary later.
After the visit Write down confirmed next steps, the follow-up date, and any monitoring your clinician asked you to do.

Build a one-page dose snapshot

The NHS appointment guide recommends making notes about what you want to discuss and bringing a list of medicines. For an ADHD medication review, gather the basics:

  • Current medication name, prescribed dose, and usual time
  • Recent taken or skipped dose records
  • Any scheduled dose that was still unhandled when you checked
  • Actual taken times, late reminder notes, or uncertainty written as context
  • Refill or supply problems
  • Side effects, symptoms, or new concerns you want to discuss

Keep the record factual. In Pillr, taken and skipped are saved dose states. A later time, uncertainty, or a dose that remains unhandled is separate context, not another saved dose state.

If the clinic sent a form or asked for a particular tracking period, follow that request first. This checklist is a prompt, not a required medical record.

Add only the context that helps

One short line can explain why a day looked different without turning your notes into a diary:

  • "Back-to-back meetings and forgot lunch"
  • "Reminder fired while driving, logged later"
  • "Dose was still unhandled when I checked at lunch"
  • "Could not fill prescription on time"

These notes can help your clinician see whether the question is about the medication, the reminder setup, sleep, meals, refills, or a disrupted routine.

Prepare questions before the call starts

Write two or three questions in advance. They might cover missed-dose instructions, side effects, refill timing, sleep, appetite, focus changes, or what your clinician wants you to track before the next review.

NICE guidance says ADHD medication reviews can include how treatment works throughout the day, adverse effects, education or employment impact, missed doses, and physical or mental health changes. A short question list can keep those topics visible.

After the appointment, update the routine

When the visit ends, capture the next step while it is fresh:

  • Any follow-up appointment or refill date
  • What your clinician asked you to monitor
  • Any reminder, note, or tracking change you need to make
  • Questions to save for next time

Do not rely on "I will remember later." Make the next action visible before the day moves on.

Frequently asked questions

What should adults with ADHD prepare before a telehealth medication appointment?

Prepare your current medication list, recent taken or skipped dose history, any still-unhandled scheduled doses, side-effect or symptom notes, refill issues, and the questions you want answered.

How can I prepare the technology before an ADHD telehealth appointment?

Open the appointment link early, test the camera and microphone, charge the device, and keep headphones or a backup contact method nearby if available.

Can a dose log help during a telehealth ADHD visit?

A factual dose log can make the conversation more specific by showing what happened between visits. It does not replace clinical judgement or medical advice.

Should I change my ADHD medication before a video appointment?

Do not change medication timing, dose, or treatment based on a checklist. Ask your doctor, psychiatrist, pharmacist, or prescribing clinician what is appropriate for your prescription.

Can Pillr prepare clinical advice for my appointment?

No. Pillr can keep reminders, dose history, routine notes, and appointment preparation together, but it does not diagnose, treat, prescribe, or replace a clinician.
