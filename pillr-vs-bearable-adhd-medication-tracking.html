Pillr vs Bearable
for ADHD medication tracking

Pillr and Bearable both connect medication records with symptoms and appointment preparation. Pillr is built around an ADHD medication routine, while Bearable places medication inside a broader, highly customisable health journal.

Published September 10, 2026 · Last reviewed September 10, 2026

A note from Pillr

We make Pillr, so this is not an independent review. This page explains how we compare Bearable with Pillr for ADHD medication reminders, dose history, symptoms, and appointment preparation.

Quick answer

Choose Pillr if your main need is an ADHD-specific medication routine with scheduled and as-needed dose records, estimated focus windows, quick reflections, pill counts, refill alerts, and medication history for appointments.

Choose Bearable if medication is one part of a wider daily journal for custom symptoms, mood, pain, energy, sleep, habits, treatments, and personal health correlations.

Device and storage preferences may decide the choice. Pillr's current listing covers iPhone and iPad, does not require a Pillr account, and keeps medication records and checked recovery copies on the device. Bearable has current iPhone and Android listings, and its account system supports encrypted server backup and cross-device restore.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone choosing whether medication should be the centre of an ADHD-specific routine or one part of a wider health journal. It is especially useful when reminders, as-needed records, focus windows, symptoms, side effects, refills, reports, privacy, or Android access will decide the fit.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, setting a medical schedule, deciding whether medication caused a symptom, or interpreting a correlation as proof.

Key differences

Pillr is medication-routine led. It centres the day on scheduled, overdue, taken, skipped, snoozed, and as-needed medication records. Estimated focus windows, reflections, pill supply, refill alerts, and appointment notes sit around that record.

Bearable is health-journal led. Its medication guide connects reminders and dose entries with symptoms, side-effect groups, mood, pain, habits, reports, and comparison graphs.

The practical choice is not which app has more features. It is whether you want a focused medication timeline or a flexible daily journal that can track many parts of health together.

Detailed comparison by use case

If reminders and dose states are the main need

Pillr separates scheduled medication from as-needed Cabinet medication. Scheduled doses can be taken, skipped, or snoozed, while as-needed medication is logged only when taken. The timeline keeps the saved schedule separate from the actual logged time.

Bearable lets you set several reminders for an individual medication on selected days, then record the time, dose, and number of doses. Its September 2026 iPhone release also added medication logging outside a schedule.

Neither reminder system can tell you what to do after a missed dose. Follow the instructions from your prescriber or pharmacist.

If focus, symptoms, or side effects matter

Pillr's Focus Timeline estimates a stimulant window from the timing you save and the dose you log. Reflections can keep mood, focus, symptoms, and side-effect notes beside medication history.

Bearable goes wider. It supports custom symptom groups, mood, pain, energy, sleep, habits, treatments, health measurements, reports, and comparison graphs. That may fit better when ADHD medication is only one factor in a larger health record.

Both views organise observations. They do not prove that medication caused, improved, or worsened a symptom. The side-effect tracking guide explains how to keep those notes factual for a healthcare conversation.

If pill supply or appointment preparation matters

Pillr tracks total pills, remaining stock, pills used per dose, low-stock thresholds, and separate refill periods. It can export medication history as CSV or PDF and keep a local My Appointment record for questions, notes, recording, transcription, and a user-reviewed summary.

Bearable offers reports, timelines, comparison graphs, and export across its wider health journal. Its current medication guide does not describe a dedicated pill-count or refill workflow, so verify that separately if supply tracking is essential.

For either app, check that the report includes the dates, dose details, symptoms, and notes your appointment needs. Pillr's appointment preparation guide can help you decide what to record.

If privacy, accounts, or device support matter

Pillr requires no Pillr account. Medication records, reflections, appointment content, and checked recovery copies stay on the device. Pillr does not offer medication cloud backup or syncing; its current privacy page explains the optional services separately.

Bearable's privacy policy describes an account using an email address, automatic backup to its servers, cross-device restore, encrypted health data, analytics, and account deletion. Bearable says it does not sell personal data.

Pillr's current listing covers iPhone and iPad. Bearable's current listings cover iPhone and Android. Review the current store, account, backup, deletion, export, analytics, and sharing details before entering a full medication history.

Where Pillr fits better

  • You want an ADHD-specific medication timeline with clear taken and skipped dose states.
  • You want scheduled and as-needed medication kept in distinct workflows.
  • You want estimated focus windows and short reflections beside medication history.
  • You want pill counts, refill alerts, CSV/PDF medication exports, and appointment preparation in the same focused app.
  • You prefer no product account and on-device medication records.

Where Bearable fits better

  • You want a highly customisable journal for symptoms, mood, pain, energy, sleep, habits, and medication.
  • You want reports and comparison graphs across many health factors.
  • You use Android or need an account-backed restore path across devices.
  • You want medication to sit inside a broad chronic-health or wellbeing record.

Why Pillr is a strong fit for an ADHD medication routine

Pillr keeps the main medication decisions visible without turning the app into a broad health dashboard. You can see what is due, record taken or skipped doses, keep as-needed medication in the Cabinet, review an estimated Focus Timeline, add reflections, track supply, and prepare a medication history for an appointment.

That narrower focus is useful when remembering and reviewing ADHD medication is the main job. If the main job is exploring symptoms, pain, sleep, habits, and other health factors together, Bearable may be the more natural fit.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app is currently available on every phone or tablet you use.
  • Whether its reminders match the schedule provided by your prescriber or pharmacist.
  • How scheduled, actual-time, skipped, corrected, and unscheduled entries appear in history.
  • Whether pill counts, refill alerts, longer history, reports, insights, or exports need paid access.
  • Whether your appointment needs a focused medication record or a wider symptom and health report.
  • How account creation, deletion, backup, recovery, analytics, export, and health-data connections work.

Is Pillr or Bearable better for ADHD medication tracking?

Pillr may fit better if you want an ADHD-specific medication routine with scheduled and as-needed dose records, estimated focus windows, pill counts, refill alerts, reflections, and appointment preparation. Bearable may fit better if you want medication inside a wider journal for symptoms, mood, pain, sleep, habits, and personal correlations.

Do Pillr and Bearable include medication reminders?

Yes. Pillr combines scheduled reminders with taken, skipped, snoozed, and as-needed medication records. Bearable supports custom medication reminders on selected days, and its current iPhone release also supports logging medication outside a schedule.

Which app is better for side-effect notes?

Pillr keeps mood, focus, symptoms, and side-effect reflections close to ADHD medication history. Bearable offers a more customisable health journal with symptom groups, reports, and comparison graphs. Neither app can determine whether medication caused a symptom.

Do Pillr or Bearable replace medical advice?

No. Both are tracking and record-keeping tools. Questions about doses, timing, side effects, symptoms, or missed medication need advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
