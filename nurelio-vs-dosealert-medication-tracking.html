Nurelio vs DoseAlert
for family medication tracking

Nurelio and DoseAlert both support medication routines across iPhone and Android, including reminders, dose records, supply tracking, reports, and optional family support. Nurelio is the broader medication, appointment, health-reading, and companion organiser. DoseAlert is the more focused reminder and caregiver-alert app.

Published October 4, 2026 · Last reviewed October 4, 2026

A note from Pillr

This article is published by Pillr. We make a medication tracking app, but this page compares Nurelio and DoseAlert for people deciding between those two options.

Quick answer

Choose Nurelio if you want a broader family health organiser with a digital weekly pillbox, appointments, selected health readings, doctor reports, and granular sharing with several companions.

Choose DoseAlert if you want a focused medication reminder with escalating alert choices, flexible schedules, paired caregiver updates, family profiles, label scanning, dose reports, and a one-time purchase rather than a subscription.

Both apps separate local medication use from optional online sharing. Their account, cloud, privacy, and paid-feature boundaries differ, so check the exact setup you plan to use.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for people choosing a medication app for themselves, a parent, a partner, or another family member. It is most useful when reminder escalation, remote check-ins, appointments, supply, reports, privacy, pricing structure, or cross-platform support will decide the fit.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, creating a taper, setting medical timing, interpreting an adherence view, or deciding what to do after a missed dose.

Key differences

Nurelio is broader family health coordination. Its current official site, App Store page, and Google Play page describe medication reminders, a weekly pillbox, supply, appointments, health readings, doctor reports, and paid companion access. Current companion permissions can include adding medication and restocking supply.

DoseAlert is reminder and caregiver-alert led. Its current official site, App Store page, and Google Play page describe escalating reminders, flexible schedules, grouped medications, family profiles, label scanning, supply records, reports, and optional paired caregiver updates.

The practical choice is whether you need one wider shared health organiser or a more focused reminder record with a simpler caregiver check-in.

Detailed comparison by use case

If reminder escalation matters most

Nurelio describes configurable stages for medication alarms, including supported critical-alert behaviour. It also keeps regular and as-needed medication separate and can notify companions about overdue intakes when sharing is enabled.

DoseAlert gives each medication up to four reminder levels. Its reminder guide describes quiet, sound, alarm, and caregiver choices that stop after the person responds. Later can create a follow-up, while Taken, Skip, and Not sure keep the dose state explicit.

No app can guarantee that every operating-system alert or remote caregiver update will arrive exactly on time. Test notification, sound, Focus, battery, data-connection, and device settings before relying on either setup.

If family or caregiver access decides the choice

Nurelio Premium lists up to four companions, end-to-end encrypted sharing, and separate permissions for medication, appointment, and health information. Its current release adds editing actions for authorised companions, including adding medication and restocking supply.

DoseAlert supports local profiles for several people and can pair two phones for selected dose status and overdue alerts. Its caregiver check runs when the patient app is online and able to process the unresolved dose, so it should be treated as a check-in signal rather than emergency monitoring.

Before inviting anyone, check who can view or edit each record, how access is revoked, what happens when a phone is offline, and what remains after unpairing.

If supply, appointments, or reports matter

Nurelio shows how long supply may last, warns before it runs low, supports refill planning, and includes a digital weekly pillbox. It also keeps appointments and selected health readings beside medication records, then creates a doctor report for PDF, QR, or encrypted sharing.

DoseAlert updates inventory from logged doses, groups medicines taken together, provides refill reminders, and exports PDF or CSV reports. Its local-first record is narrower than Nurelio's, but that can be useful if medication reminders and dose follow-through are the main job.

For practical ways to check the record behind a supply estimate, see the refill and pill-count guide. For a concise appointment record, see the medication appointment guide.

If privacy or account setup matters

Nurelio's privacy policy says medication and health records are local by default, family sharing is end-to-end encrypted, and optional backups use a private iCloud or Google Drive folder. The personal workflow can be used without signing in, while family sync uses Apple or Google sign-in. Optional AI features have separate external-processing terms.

DoseAlert's privacy overview says local medication records, reminders, inventory, and history stay on the device. Optional caregiver sharing relays selected alert details and dose status through Firebase, and the developer describes technical crash reports separately. There is no visible email sign-up for the local workflow.

Both products have simple local-use claims and more detailed boundaries for optional online features. Read the current store and privacy disclosures for the exact features you will enable.

Where Nurelio fits better

  • You want medication, appointments, health readings, and doctor reports in one place.
  • You want several companions with selected viewing or editing permissions.
  • You want a digital weekly pillbox and wider family coordination.
  • You are comfortable with a subscription for family and other advanced features.

Where DoseAlert fits better

  • You want up to four reminder levels and a focused caregiver alert.
  • You want flexible schedules, family profiles, label scanning, supply, and reports without a broader health dashboard.
  • You prefer a one-time unlock after a free trial instead of an ongoing subscription.
  • You want personal medication use without an email account.

When neither app may be enough

If you need an ADHD-focused dose timeline with estimated focus windows, structured medication reflections, refill periods, exports, and appointment preparation, Pillr is a separate option to consider. For another family-oriented comparison, see Nurelio vs DoseKeeper. For broader symptom and health journals, see CareClinic vs Guava.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app supports the exact schedule already provided by the prescriber or pharmacist.
  • How Taken, Later, Skipped, Not sure, missed, corrected, and unrecorded doses appear in history.
  • Whether the reminder style works with notification, sound, Focus, battery, alarm, and data settings on every device.
  • What happens to caregiver alerts when the patient phone is offline or the app cannot process an event.
  • Which family, AI, scanner, report, backup, and sharing features need paid access.
  • Who can view or edit medication, appointment, health, and emergency information.
  • What leaves the device for sharing, backup, crash reporting, health integrations, or optional AI features.
  • Whether the current store price and operating-system requirements fit every planned phone or tablet.

Is Nurelio or DoseAlert better for family medication tracking?

Nurelio may fit better if you want medication, appointments, health readings, a digital pillbox, and granular sharing with several companions in one app. DoseAlert may fit better if escalating reminders, a focused caregiver alert, flexible schedules, and a one-time purchase matter more.

Do Nurelio and DoseAlert work on iPhone and Android?

Yes. Both apps currently have App Store and Google Play listings. Nurelio is listed for iPhone and iPad, while DoseAlert is also listed for iPhone and iPad. Check each current store listing for operating-system requirements before choosing.

How do Nurelio and DoseAlert caregiver features differ?

Nurelio Premium describes end-to-end encrypted sharing with up to four companions and permissions for selected medication, appointment, and health information. DoseAlert pairs a caregiver phone for selected dose status and overdue alerts through Firebase when the patient phone is online and the app can process the event.

Do Nurelio or DoseAlert replace medical advice?

No. They are reminder and record-keeping tools. Questions about doses, timing, side effects, symptoms, interactions, missed medication, or refill decisions need advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
