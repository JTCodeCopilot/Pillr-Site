Apple Health vs MyTherapy
for medication reminders

Apple Health Medications and MyTherapy both support medication reminders and records. The practical difference is scope: Apple Health is built into the Apple health system, while MyTherapy adds a cross-platform medication and health journal with refill support, symptoms, measurements, family profiles, and reports.

Published July 29, 2026 · Last reviewed August 29, 2026

A note from Pillr

This article is published by Pillr. We make a medication tracking app, but this page compares Apple Health Medications and MyTherapy for people deciding between those two options.

Quick answer

Choose Apple Health Medications if you use Apple devices and want a built-in tool for schedules, reminders, follow-up alerts, taken or skipped records, as-needed logging, history, and Apple Watch access.

Choose MyTherapy if you want medication reminders beside refill tracking, symptoms, pain, mood, measurements, injection sites, family profiles, health reports, or Android support.

Apple Health is a built-in Apple feature. MyTherapy can be downloaded free on iPhone, iPad, and Android and lists ads or paid options in its store pages. Check current terms and device requirements in your region before deciding.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone choosing between a simple medication record already available on Apple devices and a separate medication app with broader treatment-journal tools.

It is especially useful if your decision depends on Apple Watch logging, Android support, refill alerts, family profiles, symptoms, measurements, or appointment reports. It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, or deciding what to do after a missed dose.

Key differences

Apple Health Medications is built into a wider Apple health record. Apple's current medication guide describes schedules, dose and follow-up reminders, taken or skipped logging, as-needed medication, history, Apple Watch access, sharing, and medication-list PDF export.

MyTherapy is a dedicated medication and treatment journal. Its current app page combines medication reminders and intake records with supply alerts, symptoms, pain, mood, measurements, injection sites, appointments, and reports.

The choice is not simply built-in versus downloaded. It is whether a focused Apple medication record is enough or whether you want refill planning and a wider day-to-day health journal in the same medication app.

Detailed comparison by use case

If reminders and a clear dose record are the main job

Apple Health covers scheduled and as-needed medication, taken or skipped records, normal reminders, and a follow-up reminder after an unlogged scheduled dose. It can also log medication from Apple Watch.

MyTherapy provides medication alarms, a medication plan, intake history, notes, and refill or prescription reminders. Before choosing, check how its current notification flow handles postponing, late entries, and unrecorded doses on your device.

If refill planning matters

MyTherapy is the clearer fit. Its current product pages list a pill counter and reminders for refills or new prescriptions.

Apple's current Medications guidance covers schedules, reminders, logs, history, sharing, and medication-list export, but does not describe pill-count or refill alerts. That boundary matters if supply planning is part of the same daily routine.

If symptoms and measurements belong beside medication

MyTherapy puts symptoms, pain, mood, blood pressure, weight, blood sugar, and other measurements into its health journal. It also lists injection-site tracking and reminders for measurements and appointments.

Apple Health can hold many types of health information, but its Medications feature remains a distinct medication schedule and history. Consider whether seeing records in the same wider Health app is enough or whether you want them presented as one treatment journal.

If family or sharing features matter

Apple Health lets users share selected Health data, including medication information, with trusted people through Apple's sharing controls.

MyTherapy lists family profiles for managing several medication and health routines in one account. It also describes health reports and a temporary one-time code for sharing progress. Check current profile limits, consent, and paid-feature rules before setting up a household.

If privacy and account setup will decide the choice

Apple says Health data is encrypted when the device is locked and that users control which Health data is stored or shared. Optional account-based syncing has its own security setup.

MyTherapy says it can be used with or without an account. Its privacy policy describes encrypted transfer to a central European Union database, analytics, optional advertising consent, and separate consent for some partner features. Read the current policy for the setup you plan to use.

Where Apple Health fits better

  • You want medication reminders and history without setting up a separate medication app.
  • You already use Apple Health and want medication data inside the same system.
  • You want to log taken or skipped medication from Apple Watch.
  • You want a simple medication-list PDF or Apple Health sharing workflow.

Where MyTherapy fits better

  • You need Android support or want the same app category across iPhone and Android.
  • You want pill counts, refill alerts, or prescription reminders.
  • You want symptoms, pain, mood, measurements, or injection sites beside medication records.
  • You want family profiles or broader health reports for appointments.

When neither app may be enough

You may need a different tool if your priority is a provider-managed plan, a specific pharmacy connection, a caregiver alert after an unrecorded dose, or a specialist report that neither option produces clearly.

If neither app fits the way you track ADHD medication, Pillr is built around reminders, dose history, estimated focus windows, reflections, refill planning, and medication notes. It is a separate option, not part of the Apple Health and MyTherapy comparison above.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether every reminder and schedule type you need is available on your device and operating-system version.
  • How each option shows taken, skipped, late, as-needed, and unrecorded medication.
  • Whether refill, family, report, and ad-free features are free in your region.
  • Whether the current report includes the dates and details you need for appointments.
  • How Health, MyTherapy, syncing, analytics, advertising, and partner features handle your information.
  • Whether moving to another device requires an Apple or MyTherapy account.

Is Apple Health or MyTherapy better for medication reminders?

Apple Health Medications may fit better if you want a built-in Apple tool for schedules, follow-up reminders, taken or skipped records, and Apple Watch logging. MyTherapy may fit better if you want Android support, refill reminders, family profiles, health measurements, symptoms, and reports in the same app.

Can Apple Health and MyTherapy both keep medication records?

Yes. Apple Health keeps scheduled and as-needed medication history with taken or skipped records. MyTherapy keeps a medication plan and intake history alongside health-journal entries.

Which app supports refill reminders?

MyTherapy currently lists a pill counter plus refill and prescription reminders. Apple's current Medications guidance describes schedules, dose reminders, logging, history, and medication-list export, but does not list pill-count or refill alerts.

Do Apple Health or MyTherapy replace medical advice?

No. They are tracking and reminder tools. Questions about doses, timing, side effects, interactions, and missed medication need advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
