Apple Health Medications vs Guava
for medication tracking

Apple Health Medications and Guava can both keep medication reminders and dose records on Apple devices. Apple Health is the built-in, focused option, while Guava adds pill supply, symptoms, connected medical records, appointment preparation, Android, and web access.

Published August 19, 2026 · Last reviewed August 26, 2026

A note from Pillr

This article is published by Pillr. We make a medication tracking app, but this page compares Apple Health Medications and Guava for people deciding between those two options.

Quick answer

Choose Apple Health Medications if you use Apple devices and want a built-in tool for schedules, reminders, follow-up alerts, taken or skipped records, as-needed logging, medication history, and Apple Watch access.

Choose Guava if you want medication reminders beside pill counts, refill alerts, symptoms, mood, pain, medical records, patient portals, device connections, and visit preparation. Guava also has iPhone, iPad, Android, and web access.

Guava's current iPhone release can sync medications and dose events from Apple Health. That can make Guava a wider record around Apple Health rather than a completely separate starting point, but check the current permissions and exactly what moves before relying on the connection.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone deciding whether Apple's built-in medication record is enough or whether a broader personal health record would be more useful.

It is especially relevant if your choice depends on Apple Watch logging, pill supply, refill reminders, symptoms, medical records, patient portals, Android access, or appointment preparation. It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, or deciding what to do after a missed dose.

Key differences

Apple Health Medications is focused and built in. Apple's current Medications guide covers schedules, reminders, follow-up alerts, taken or skipped logging, as-needed medication, history, Apple Watch access, sharing, and medication-list PDF export.

Guava is a wider personal health record. Its current medication guide combines reminders and dose entries with pill counts, refill alerts, symptoms, medical records, supported patient portals, and visit preparation.

The two options can now connect. Guava's current iPhone release notes say it can sync medications and dose events from Apple Health. The practical decision may be whether Apple Health alone is enough or whether Guava adds useful context around the same medication record.

Detailed comparison by use case

If reminders and a clear dose record are the main job

Apple Health covers scheduled and as-needed medication, taken or skipped records, normal reminders, and a follow-up reminder after an unlogged scheduled dose. It can also log medication from Apple Watch.

Guava supports flexible medication schedules, reminders, completed entries, notes, and medication history inside a wider health record. Before choosing, check how the current reminder flow handles postponing, late entries, and anything left unrecorded.

If refill planning matters

Guava is the clearer fit. It lists remaining pill counts, refill reminders, and medication supply inside the same record.

Apple's current Medications guidance covers schedules, reminders, logs, history, sharing, and medication-list export, but does not describe pill-count or refill alerts.

If symptoms and records belong beside medication

Guava can keep symptoms, mood, pain, measurements, medical records, lab results, doctor notes, documents, and supported patient-portal data alongside medication. Its Visit Prep tools are designed to organise that wider record before an appointment.

Apple Health can hold many types of health data, but Medications remains a distinct medication schedule and history. Consider whether keeping everything inside Health is enough or whether you want Guava to bring those records into one appointment-focused view.

If you already use Apple Health

You do not necessarily need to start again in Guava. The current iPhone release says Guava can sync medications and dose events from Apple Health.

Check the current version, permissions, which fields are included, and what happens when a record changes in either place. A connection should not be treated as a substitute for checking the medication history you rely on.

If privacy and account setup will decide the choice

Apple says Health data is encrypted when the device is locked and that users control which Health data is stored or shared. Optional iCloud syncing for Apple Health depends on the user's Apple security setup.

Guava requires an account and uses cloud services for its wider record and connections. Its privacy and security page says data is encrypted, users control sharing, and Guava does not sell health information. Read the current policy before connecting records or portals.

Where Apple Health fits better

  • You want medication reminders and history without setting up a separate medication app.
  • You already use Apple Health and want medication data inside the same system.
  • You want to log taken or skipped medication from Apple Watch.
  • You want a focused medication history and medication-list PDF.

Where Guava fits better

  • You want pill counts and refill reminders beside dose records.
  • You want symptoms, mood, pain, records, labs, or device data in the same wider history.
  • You want patient-portal connections or Visit Prep for appointments.
  • You need Android or web access as well as Apple-device support.

When neither app may be enough

You may need a different tool if your priority is a provider-managed plan, a particular pharmacy connection, a caregiver alert after an unrecorded dose, or a specialist report that neither option produces clearly.

If neither app fits the way you track ADHD medication, Pillr is built around reminders, dose history, estimated focus windows, reflections, refill planning, and medication notes. It is a separate option, not part of the Apple Health and Guava comparison above.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether every reminder and schedule type you need is available on your device and operating-system version.
  • How each option shows taken, skipped, late, as-needed, changed, and unrecorded medication.
  • Exactly which medication fields and dose events Guava currently syncs from Apple Health.
  • Whether pill-count, refill, Visit Prep, record-connection, and export features are available in your plan and region.
  • How Health, iCloud, Guava, patient portals, and connected devices handle your information.
  • Whether the current history or report includes the dates and details you need for appointments.
