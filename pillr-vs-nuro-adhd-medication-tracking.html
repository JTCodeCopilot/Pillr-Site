Pillr vs Nuro
for ADHD medication tracking

Pillr and Nuro are both built around ADHD medication records, timing views, and everyday context. Pillr connects reminders and factual dose history with structured reflections, refills, exports, and appointment notes. Nuro puts more emphasis on sleep, steps, caffeine, meals, water, symptoms, and visual analytics around medication.

Published August 23, 2026 · Last reviewed August 23, 2026

A note from Pillr

We make Pillr, so this is not an independent review. This page explains how we compare Nuro with Pillr for ADHD medication reminders, dose records, timing estimates, and daily tracking.

Quick answer

Choose Pillr if you want reminders, clear taken or skipped dose history, as-needed medication, estimated focus windows, structured reflections, pill counts, refill reminders, exports, and appointment notes in one local-first app.

Choose Nuro if you want an ADHD medication tracker that puts sleep, steps, caffeine, meals, water, symptoms, side effects, and visual analytics close to its medication timeline.

Both are available on iPhone and iPad and both list a free download with optional paid features. The practical choice is between Pillr's fuller medication-routine record and Nuro's wider daily-context dashboard.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone choosing between two ADHD-focused medication trackers on Apple devices. It is especially useful if your decision turns on clear dose states, as-needed medication, sleep and lifestyle context, structured reflections, refill detail, exports, appointment preparation, accounts, or cloud storage.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, deciding when to take medication, or interpreting an app's timeline or pattern as medical proof.

Key differences

Pillr is medication-routine led. It connects reminders and a chronological dose record with a user-adjusted Focus Timeline, structured reflections, refill planning, history exports, and appointment preparation.

Nuro is daily-context led. Its current official site and App Store page describe medication logging and reminders beside an effect timeline, sleep and step imports, caffeine, meals, water, mood, symptoms, side effects, inventory, and visual analytics.

The overlap is strongest in ADHD medication logs, timing estimates, symptoms, side effects, and pill supply. The clearest difference is whether you want a factual medication workflow with appointment tools or a broader view of daily factors around medication.

Detailed comparison by use case

If confirming what happened is the main job

Pillr gives scheduled medication an explicit taken, skipped, upcoming, or overdue place in the day's timeline. As-needed medication is kept separately until it is logged, and the saved history can be exported as CSV or PDF.

Nuro supports quick dose logging, medication history, reminders, and a live medication view. If a specific missed, skipped, postponed, or as-needed status matters to you, check that exact action in the current app before moving your record.

If the medication timing view matters most

Both apps provide a visual estimate around a logged ADHD medication dose. Pillr's Focus Timeline uses onset, fade, and duration settings saved for the medication. Nuro describes an Effect Timeline using medication profiles that users can customise.

Neither view can know exactly when medication starts, peaks, fades, or affects you. Use any timing view as an organisational estimate and discuss medication questions with a qualified healthcare professional.

If symptoms, sleep, or daily context drive the choice

Pillr uses structured Reflection check-ins for focus, mood, symptoms, and side effects. It can also show optional Apple Health summaries for sleep, activity, distance, and heart rate inside Health & Insights.

Nuro makes wider daily context more central. Its current product information describes sleep, steps, caffeine, meals, water, mood, symptoms, side effects, and visual analytics beside the medication record. This may fit better if those signals are the main reason you want a tracker.

If refills, exports, and appointment preparation matter

Pillr tracks pill inventory in separate refill periods and can warn when the saved count reaches a user's threshold. It also offers CSV/PDF dose-history exports and a dedicated My Appointment workflow for notes, recordings, summaries, and questions.

Nuro's current release adds inventory tracking for all medications, and its help information describes a data export. Check the exact export format and paid limits if you need a repeatable report for appointments.

If privacy, accounts, and storage decide the choice

Pillr does not have a Pillr account or medication cloud-sync system. Medication records and checked recovery copies stay on the device. The Pillr privacy page explains optional AI features, medication lookup, Apple Health access, and analytics separately.

Nuro's current privacy policy describes Apple Sign-In, Supabase cloud storage, syncing, backup, and account deletion. Its current App Store privacy label separately lists identifiers under tracking and linked data, while the website policy says it does not track users across other apps or websites. Review both current disclosures before choosing.

Where Pillr fits better

  • You want explicit taken or skipped dose history and as-needed medication support.
  • You want structured reflections, refill periods, CSV/PDF exports, and appointment notes.
  • You prefer medication records to stay on the device without a Pillr account or cloud sync.
  • You want reminders, dose history, focus estimates, and follow-through in one medication-led workflow.

Where Nuro fits better

  • You want sleep, steps, caffeine, meals, water, mood, symptoms, and side effects close to the medication view.
  • You want an adjustable effect profile and visual analytics to be central to the experience.
  • You want Apple Health sleep and step imports.
  • You prefer account-based cloud syncing and backup across supported Apple devices.

Why Pillr is a strong fit for ADHD medication tracking

Pillr is strongest when the goal is to keep the medication routine itself clear: what was due, what was taken or skipped, what remains unhandled, how much supply remains, what you noticed, and what you want to bring to an appointment.

Its Focus Timeline is adjustable and intentionally presented as an estimate. Pillr does not tell you what medication to take, when to take it, or how to respond to side effects or a missed dose.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app supports your exact medication, formulation, and prescribed schedule.
  • Whether its dose history distinguishes the states you need, such as taken, skipped, postponed, or as needed.
  • How the timing profile can be changed and how clearly the app treats it as an estimate.
  • Whether sleep, steps, caffeine, meals, water, symptoms, refills, exports, or appointment notes matter to you.
  • Which features need a paid plan in your App Store region.
  • How account creation, cloud storage, backup, analytics, tracking disclosures, export, and deletion work.
  • Whether the app supports every device you plan to use.

Is Pillr or Nuro better for ADHD medication tracking?

Pillr may fit better if you want reminders, taken or skipped dose history, focus-window estimates, structured reflections, refill tracking, exports, and appointment notes in a local-first app. Nuro may fit better if sleep, step, caffeine, meal, water, and symptom context are central to the record you want.

Do Pillr and Nuro require an account?

Pillr does not require a Pillr account and keeps medication records and checked recovery copies on the device. Nuro's current privacy information describes Apple Sign-In for an account and Supabase cloud storage for data syncing and backup.

Do Pillr and Nuro know exactly when ADHD medication starts or wears off?

No app can know exactly how medication will affect a person. Both apps show timing views based on saved settings or app profiles. Treat those views as estimates and tracking aids, not medical guidance.

Are Pillr and Nuro available on Android?

No. Pillr is Apple-focused. Nuro is currently available for iPhone and iPad, while its website lists Android as a future release with a waitlist.
