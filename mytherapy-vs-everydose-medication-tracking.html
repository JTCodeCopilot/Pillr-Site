MyTherapy vs EveryDose
for medication tracking

MyTherapy and EveryDose both cover reminders, dose records, refills, and reports. MyTherapy places medication beside symptoms, measurements, appointments, and a wider health diary, while EveryDose keeps medication information, interaction alerts, progress sharing, and the daily schedule closer to the centre.

Published August 7, 2026 · Last reviewed August 7, 2026

A note from Pillr

This article is published by Pillr. We make a medication tracking app, but this page compares MyTherapy and EveryDose for people deciding between those two options.

Quick answer

Choose MyTherapy if you want medication reminders and refill tracking beside symptoms, mood, measurements, injection sites, appointments, and a broader health diary.

Choose EveryDose if you want a medication-first daily schedule with interaction alerts, medication leaflets, progress sharing, smartwatch alerts, and an optional paid family and health-tracking setup.

Both are available on iPhone and Android, both can be downloaded free, and both list optional paid features. The practical choice is whether your record starts with wider health tracking or with medication information and daily dose actions.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone choosing between two established medication reminder apps, especially when dose history, refills, symptoms, measurements, reports, medication information, or family support will decide the fit.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, interpreting an interaction alert, or deciding what to do after a missed dose.

Key differences

MyTherapy is broader-health-diary led. Its current app overview places medication intake, refills, symptoms, mood, measurements, appointments, notes, and health reports inside one ongoing record.

EveryDose is medication-information led. Its current feature page centres the daily medication schedule, taken, skipped, or snoozed actions, medication leaflets, interaction alerts, history, progress sharing, and smartwatch alerts.

The overlap is substantial. Both can support a daily medication routine. The difference is whether you also want a wide personal health diary or a more focused medication list with built-in information and sharing.

Detailed comparison by use case

If reminders and dose history are the main need

MyTherapy supports personalised reminders, a daily medication plan, taken or skipped records, notes, refill reminders, and a longer health diary. It may fit better when the same record also needs symptoms, measurements, or appointments.

EveryDose supports daily, specific-day, cycle, and as-needed schedules. Its daily view records taken, skipped, or snoozed actions, while the current iPhone release also lists every-X-hours schedules, a dedicated as-needed history, and improved reports and exports.

EveryDose now covers more interval-style routines than older versions did. Dosecast remains a separate option if reminders based specifically on the last recorded dose are central; see the Dosecast vs EveryDose comparison.

If symptoms, mood, or measurements matter

MyTherapy treats symptom, pain, mood, weight, blood pressure, and other measurement records as part of the main health diary. It also lists reminders for measurements and appointments.

EveryDose places health trackers and charts in its paid Plus experience. This may be enough if medication remains the main record and you only need a defined set of measurements beside it.

Neither app's charts prove that a medication caused or improved a symptom. Use them to organise questions for a healthcare appointment, not to change treatment yourself.

If medication information or interaction alerts matter

EveryDose lists medication leaflets, drug-to-drug interaction alerts, drug images, and food information in its standard features. This is its clearest difference from MyTherapy in the current US-facing information.

MyTherapy's medication-information tools can differ by country and programme. Check what is available in your region rather than assuming a feature shown elsewhere will appear in your app.

An in-app alert is not a replacement for advice from a pharmacist, doctor, psychiatrist, or prescribing clinician.

If reports or family support matter

MyTherapy can create health reports and a medication plan, and it describes a one-time sharing code for a doctor. Its broader diary may fit better when the report needs symptoms, mood, measurements, and medication together.

EveryDose includes progress emails for invited friends and lists a detailed medication-list PDF and history export. EveryDose Plus adds multiple profiles and more health tracking. A progress email is different from managing another person's profile, so check the exact role and permission you need.

If privacy and account setup matter

Both apps can hold sensitive medication and health information. MyTherapy's privacy policy and current store disclosures explain its data handling, analytics, advertising, app functions, and deletion choices.

EveryDose's security page describes encrypted storage and connections, US-based hosting, security reviews, and extra controls used in its services. Its store disclosures describe the data collected for app functions and related services.

Review current sign-in, deletion, export, analytics, backup, family, and sharing rules before entering a full medication history.

Where MyTherapy fits better

  • You want medication, symptoms, mood, measurements, appointments, and notes in one health diary.
  • You want injection-site tracking or measurement reminders.
  • You want a health report that combines medication with wider health entries.
  • You want iPad support as well as iPhone and Android.

Where EveryDose fits better

  • You want medication leaflets and interaction alerts close to the daily medication list.
  • You want progress emails for a trusted person.
  • You want smartwatch alerts and one-tap taken actions on supported watches.
  • You use iPhone and want every-X-hours reminders or a dedicated as-needed history.

When neither app may be enough

MyTherapy and EveryDose are broad medication-management apps. If your main need is an ADHD-specific routine with quick taken or skipped records, estimated focus windows, reflections, refill planning, and medication notes, Pillr is a separate option to consider.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app supports your exact prescribed schedule and as-needed workflow.
  • How actual intake time, skipped entries, snoozes, corrections, and quantities appear in history.
  • Which refill, family, profile, health-tracking, report, and export tools need a paid option.
  • Whether a report contains the dates, medication details, symptoms, and measurements your appointment needs.
  • Which medication information and interaction tools are available in your country.
  • How sign-in, deletion, analytics, backup, sharing, and health-data handling work.

Is MyTherapy or EveryDose better for medication tracking?

MyTherapy may fit better if you want medication records beside symptoms, mood, measurements, injection sites, appointments, and a broader health diary. EveryDose may fit better if medication information, interaction alerts, progress sharing, smartwatch alerts, and a medication-first daily schedule matter more.

Do MyTherapy and EveryDose include medication reminders?

Yes. Both apps support medication schedules, reminders, taken or skipped records, dose history, and refill tools. Their schedule types, reminder actions, paid limits, and report formats differ.

Which app offers the better report?

MyTherapy may fit better when a report needs medication, symptoms, mood, and measurements together. EveryDose lists progress emails, a medication-list PDF, and history export. Check the exact fields and sharing method before choosing.

Do MyTherapy or EveryDose replace medical advice?

No. They are reminder and record-keeping tools. Questions about doses, timing, interactions, side effects, symptoms, and missed medication need advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
