Pillr vs Medication Timer
for medication tracking

Pillr and Medication Timer both support reminders, dose records, as-needed medication, and reports. Pillr is a native Apple app centred on ADHD medication history, focus windows, reflections, refills, and appointment preparation. Medication Timer is a wider cloud household organiser with family profiles, health readings, symptoms, and access across Apple, Android, and web.

Published November 9, 2026 · Last reviewed November 9, 2026

A note from Pillr

We make Pillr, so this is not an independent review. This page explains how we compare Medication Timer with Pillr for ADHD medication tracking, household routines, reminders, reports, privacy, and everyday follow-through.

Quick answer

Choose Pillr if you want a native Apple app focused on a personal ADHD medication record: scheduled and as-needed doses, actual taken times, estimated focus windows, structured reflections, refill periods, CSV/PDF exports, and appointment preparation.

Choose Medication Timer if you want one signed-in household record across Apple, Android, and web, with scheduled routines, as-needed timers, family profiles, selected health readings, symptoms, reports, and paid sharing with additional carers.

The practical difference is a focused personal ADHD medication timeline versus a broader cloud household health record. Neither app should decide whether a dose is appropriate or medically safe.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone choosing between a focused personal ADHD medication tracker and a broader household medication organiser. It is especially useful when dose history, focus-window records, family coordination, symptoms, health readings, appointment preparation, storage, pricing structure, or device support will decide the fit.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, creating a schedule, deciding what to do after a missed dose, or using an app timer as proof that another dose is safe.

Key differences

Pillr is ADHD-medication-record led. It connects native reminders and a chronological dose history with an estimated Focus Timeline, structured reflections, refill periods, exports, and appointment preparation.

Medication Timer is household-health-record led. Its current official site, App Store page, and Google Play page describe scheduled routines, as-needed timers, family profiles, health readings, symptoms, reports, and access across supported apps and the web.

The overlap is strongest around reminders, dose records, as-needed medication, supply, and reports. The clearest decision is whether you want a personal ADHD review record or a wider signed-in household system.

Detailed comparison by use case

If ADHD history and reflection matter

Pillr keeps scheduled, overdue, taken, skipped, corrected, and as-needed medication in one timeline. It can record the time a dose was actually taken, estimate a stimulant focus window from timing saved by the user, and pair the dose with medication-benefit and side-effect-impact ratings plus optional notes.

Medication Timer records medication routines and adds symptoms, temperature, selected health readings, trends, and reports. That broader context may suit general household health tracking, while Pillr offers the more focused ADHD dose-review workflow.

If family or caregiver coordination matters

Pillr is built around the person's own medication history. It supports shareable CSV/PDF exports and appointment preparation, but it is not a live household cloud-sharing system.

Medication Timer supports several household members inside one cloud account. Its paid Family Care features add sharing with additional carers, longer history, trends, and reports. Check who owns the account, who can edit each record, and how access is removed before inviting anyone.

If reminders and as-needed records matter

Pillr connects scheduled alerts, snooze, follow-ups, and optional Adaptive Reminders to the medication timeline. Cabinet medication can be logged as needed without a routine schedule, and the record can use the actual time taken.

Medication Timer combines scheduled routines, reminders, overdue items, widgets, supported watch alerts, and as-needed timers. These tools record a plan and events; they cannot determine medically safe timing.

If privacy, accounts, and storage decide the choice

Pillr has no Pillr account or medication cloud-sync system. Medication records and checked recovery copies stay on the device. The Pillr privacy page explains medication lookup, optional AI features, Apple Health, and product analytics separately.

Medication Timer's privacy policy describes an account-based service in which most household medication and health information is stored in the cloud. It describes encryption in transit and at rest, service-provider processing, and account deletion.

Choose the storage model you are comfortable maintaining. A cloud household can make cross-device access and sharing easier; an on-device personal record reduces how much medication information needs to leave the device.

Where Pillr fits better

  • You want a native Apple app with clear ADHD medication history.
  • You want estimated focus windows, structured reflections, and appointment preparation.
  • You want separate refill periods and CSV/PDF medication-history exports.
  • You do not want a product account or medication cloud-sync system.

Where Medication Timer fits better

  • You want one household record across Apple, Android, and web.
  • You want symptoms, temperature, selected health readings, and medication together.
  • You want several family members and paid sharing with additional carers.
  • You are comfortable using a signed-in cloud account for medication and household health information.

Why Pillr is a strong fit for ADHD medication tracking

Pillr is strongest when the daily ADHD medication record is the main job: what was due, what was taken or skipped, what remains unhandled, the estimated focus window, what you noticed afterward, how much supply remains, and what you want to bring to an appointment.

That focused native workflow may fit better than a household health organiser when personal dose history, reflections, and later review matter as much as receiving the reminder.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app supports the exact schedule already provided by the prescriber or pharmacist.
  • How taken, snoozed, skipped, missed, corrected, unrecorded, and as-needed doses appear in history.
  • Whether reminders work with notification, sound, Focus, battery, watch, data, and background settings on every planned device.
  • Which medication, history, reflection, refill, report, health-reading, household, caregiver, and sharing features need paid access.
  • Whether you are comfortable with an on-device personal record or a signed-in cloud household record.
  • Who can view or edit each family member's medication and health information.
  • Whether the current store price, plan terms, and operating-system requirements fit your devices.

Is Pillr or Medication Timer better for medication tracking?

Pillr may fit better if you want a native Apple app centred on ADHD dose history, estimated focus windows, structured reflections, refills, exports, and appointment preparation. Medication Timer may fit better if you want a signed-in household record across Apple, Android, and web with family profiles, selected health readings, symptoms, and shared care features.

How do Pillr and Medication Timer differ for reminders?

Pillr connects scheduled reminders, snooze, follow-ups, and optional adaptive timing to its ADHD medication timeline. Medication Timer combines scheduled routines, reminders, as-needed timers, widgets, and supported watch alerts with a broader household health record.

Do Pillr and Medication Timer require accounts?

Pillr does not require a Pillr account and keeps medication records and checked recovery copies on the device. Medication Timer uses a signed-in cloud account so supported devices, household members, and sharing features can work together.

Do Pillr or Medication Timer replace medical advice?

No. They are reminder and record-keeping tools. Questions about doses, timing, side effects, symptoms, interactions, missed medication, or as-needed use need advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
