Nurelio vs DoseKeeper
for medication tracking

Nurelio and DoseKeeper both combine medication reminders with dose history, supply tracking, and reports. Nurelio centres a cross-platform personal and family medication organiser with a digital pillbox, appointments, and health readings. DoseKeeper centres private medication management on Apple devices with blister guidance, batch inventory, expiry tracking, diary notes, and detailed PDF reports.

Published September 28, 2026 · Last reviewed October 4, 2026

A note from Pillr

This article is published by Pillr. We make a medication tracking app, but this page compares Nurelio and DoseKeeper for people deciding between those two options.

Quick answer

Choose Nurelio if you want medication reminders on iPhone, iPad, or Android, a digital weekly pillbox, appointments and health readings in the same record, or paid sharing with family members who can follow selected information.

Choose DoseKeeper if you use recent Apple devices and want batch-based inventory, blister guidance, expiry alerts, grouped reminders, Apple Watch logging, NFC medication tags, a wider health diary, or detailed PDF reports.

Both can be used free with paid options, but the boundaries differ. Nurelio describes its personal workflow as free and reserves family, AI, travel, and some scanning tools for paid access. DoseKeeper limits its free tier to three medicines and two scheduled doses, then unlocks higher limits and advanced tools through DoseKeeper Pass.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone choosing between a cross-platform personal or family medication organiser and a private Apple-focused medication manager. It is especially useful when caregiver access, a weekly pillbox, supply and expiry tracking, appointments, reports, Apple Watch use, privacy, or device compatibility will decide the fit.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, setting medical timing, interpreting an adherence view, or deciding what to do after a missed dose.

Key differences

Nurelio is personal and family coordination led. Its current official site, App Store page, and Google Play page describe medication reminders, a weekly pillbox, stock, appointments, health readings, doctor reports, and paid companion access across Apple and Android devices.

DoseKeeper is Apple medication-management led. Its current official site and App Store page describe flexible reminders, grouped dose logging, blister guidance, batch inventory, expiry alerts, a health diary, Apple Watch logging, NFC tags, and detailed PDF reports.

The practical choice is whether you need medication coordination across people and phone platforms, or deeper inventory and reporting tools inside the Apple ecosystem.

Detailed comparison by use case

If reminders and dose follow-through matter most

Nurelio describes configurable multi-stage medication alarms, supported critical alerts on iOS, one-tap Taken, Later, and Skipped actions, and separate regular and as-needed medication. Its current release also lets companions with edit permission add medication and top up supply for the person they support.

DoseKeeper supports different days and times, interval planning, food-related context, missed-dose follow-ups, grouped reminders, supported critical alerts, and optional post-intake alarms. Apple Watch and App Shortcuts provide additional ways to review and record supported medication actions.

No app can guarantee that every operating-system alert will arrive exactly on time. Test the chosen setup with notification, sound, Focus, battery, and device settings.

If supply, pillboxes, or expiry dates matter

Nurelio shows how long medication supply may last, warns before stock runs low, supports refill reminders, and presents a digital weekly pillbox with a guided filling workflow.

DoseKeeper organises supply into batches, tracks expiry dates, shows blister progress, estimates remaining units, and creates refill forecasts. Its current inventory guide notes that some medication forms are not reduced automatically when they cannot be represented reliably as discrete units.

For more context on checking the record behind a supply estimate, see the refill and pill-count guide.

If family support decides the choice

Nurelio Premium lists up to four companions, granular sharing of medication, appointment, or health information, and overdue-intake notifications. Its current App Store release adds an edit permission that can let a companion add medication and restock supply.

DoseKeeper supports filtered reports for selected people, but its current public information does not describe a comparable live caregiver-sharing or companion-sync workflow. If another person needs ongoing access, Nurelio is the clearer fit between these two apps.

Before sharing sensitive information, check who can see and edit each category, how access is revoked, what reaches a server, and what remains after a connection is removed.

If appointments and reports matter

Nurelio keeps doctor contacts and appointments beside medication and health readings. It can generate a doctor report locally and share it as a PDF, QR handoff, or encrypted link. Availability of pharmacy and doctor-portal tools can vary by country.

DoseKeeper creates PDFs for medication logs, inventory, trends, scheduled-dose insights, diary entries, and selected blood-pressure workflows. Its report guide describes filters for medicines, people, and date ranges.

If you are preparing for a review, decide whether you need appointment organisation or a more detailed medication and inventory export. The medication appointment guide explains how to prepare a concise record without making treatment decisions.

If privacy, accounts, or platforms decide the choice

Nurelio's privacy policy says medication and health records are local by default, family sharing is end-to-end encrypted, and optional backups use a private iCloud or Google Drive folder. The site says personal use works without an account, while the current terms and privacy policy describe Apple or Google sign-in for family sync and some premium or cloud features.

Nurelio's optional Premium AI features have a different boundary. Its privacy policy says selected chat, voice, lab-report, receipt, or appointment-card content can be sent to named external AI providers when the user starts those features. People who do not use the AI tools do not send that content through the AI workflow.

DoseKeeper's privacy page says the app does not collect app data, has no advertising or behaviour tracking, and requests selected Apple Health information only when a user creates a supported blood-pressure report. Apple's current privacy label says Data Not Collected.

Where Nurelio fits better

  • You want iPhone, iPad, and Android support.
  • You want a digital weekly pillbox, appointments, health readings, and doctor reports together.
  • You want paid companion access with configurable viewing or editing permissions.
  • You want the personal medication workflow to remain available without a product account.

Where DoseKeeper fits better

  • You want blister guidance, batch inventory, expiry tracking, and refill forecasts.
  • You want Apple Watch logging, NFC medication tags, widgets, and App Shortcuts.
  • You want a wider health diary and several filtered PDF report types.
  • You prefer an Apple-focused app whose current privacy label says Data Not Collected.

When neither app may be enough

If you need an ADHD-focused timeline with explicit dose states, estimated focus windows, structured medication reflections, and appointment preparation in one record, Pillr is a separate option to consider. For other broad health-tracker choices, see CareClinic vs Guava or the reminder-focused Medisafe vs EveryDose comparison.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app supports your exact prescribed schedule and as-needed workflow.
  • How taken, later, missed, skipped, corrected, postponed, and unrecorded doses appear in history.
  • Whether the reminder style works with your device's notification, sound, Focus, battery, and alarm settings.
  • How supply corrections, refill dates, batch changes, blister progress, and expiry records are handled.
  • Which family, AI, report, diary, inventory, alarm, and device features need paid access.
  • Whether an Apple or Google sign-in is required for the exact Nurelio features you plan to use.
  • What data leaves the device for sharing, backup, health integrations, reports, or optional AI features.
  • Whether every phone, tablet, watch, or computer you plan to use meets the current system requirements.

Is Nurelio or DoseKeeper better for medication tracking?

Nurelio may fit better if cross-platform use, a digital weekly pillbox, appointments, health readings, and paid family support matter. DoseKeeper may fit better if you use recent Apple devices and want batch inventory, blister guidance, expiry tracking, Apple Watch logging, NFC tags, and detailed PDF reports.

Do Nurelio and DoseKeeper both include medication reminders?

Yes. Nurelio describes configurable, multi-stage alarms and supported critical alerts. DoseKeeper describes flexible schedules, missed-dose follow-ups, grouped reminders, and supported alarm or critical-alert options. Device settings and permissions still affect delivery.

Do Nurelio and DoseKeeper require an account?

Nurelio says its local single-person workflow can be used without an account, while its terms and privacy policy describe Apple or Google sign-in for family sync and some premium or cloud features. DoseKeeper says no sign-up is required for its on-device record.

Do Nurelio or DoseKeeper replace medical advice?

No. They are organisation and record-keeping tools. Questions about doses, timing, side effects, symptoms, interactions, missed medication, or refill decisions need advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
