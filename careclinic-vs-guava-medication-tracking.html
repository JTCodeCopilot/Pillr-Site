CareClinic vs Guava
for medication and health tracking

CareClinic and Guava both place medication records beside symptoms and wider health information. CareClinic is centred on care plans, a health diary, and caregiver access, while Guava puts connected medical records, patient portals, pill supply, and visit preparation in the same personal health record.

Published August 4, 2026 · Last reviewed August 7, 2026

A note from Pillr

This article is published by Pillr. We make a medication tracking app, but this page compares CareClinic and Guava for people deciding between those two options.

Quick answer

Choose CareClinic if you want medication and symptom tracking inside a wider care plan, especially when caregiver access, family profiles, an Apple Watch app, appointments, and a detailed daily health diary matter.

Choose Guava if you want medication reminders beside pill counts, refill planning, connected patient portals, medical records, lab results, device data, and appointment preparation.

Both are available on iPhone and Android, both use accounts to support connected health records, and both offer free access with optional paid features. The better fit depends on whether you need a care-management diary or a connected personal health record.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone who wants medication reminders and history beside symptoms, side effects, mood, pain, measurements, appointments, or medical records. It is especially useful if caregiver access, pill supply, patient portals, reports, or connected devices will decide which app fits.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, changing a dose, deciding whether a medicine caused a symptom, or interpreting a chart as medical proof.

Key differences

CareClinic is care-plan led. Its current feature overview places medication, symptoms, measurements, appointments, care teams, and reports inside one daily health diary and care plan.

Guava is personal-health-record led. Its current medication tracking guide combines reminders and pill supply with symptoms, medical records, patient portals, device data, and appointment preparation.

The overlap is substantial. Both can record medication and health changes. The practical difference is whether you want shared care routines and a diary, or connected records and a broader view of your medical history.

Detailed comparison by use case

If medication reminders are the main starting point

CareClinic supports medication schedules, reminders, dose check-ins, as-needed entries, refill reminders, and medication history inside its care plan. This may fit well when daily tasks, symptoms, measurements, and caregiver access also need to stay together.

Guava combines schedules and reminders with completed entries, pill counts, refill reminders, medication lists, and wider health records. It may fit better when the reminder is one part of a connected medical history.

If symptoms or side effects are the main record

CareClinic uses a health diary, symptom tracker, body map, mood entries, measurements, and reports to review medication beside other daily changes.

Guava also keeps symptoms, mood, pain, medication, and measurements together. Its wider record connections may be more useful if labs, doctor notes, or device data need to sit beside the symptom history.

Patterns in either app can help organise questions, but they do not prove that medication caused or improved a symptom.

If family or caregiver support matters

CareClinic describes care teams, dependent profiles, and remote monitoring for people who help manage another person's care. This is the clearer fit when shared routines and caregiver access are central.

Guava offers profile management and family options, but the practical setup is more closely tied to maintaining each person's broader health record. Check current permissions, sharing controls, and paid limits before adding sensitive family data.

If you want records for a healthcare appointment

CareClinic can organise medication, symptoms, measurements, notes, appointments, and care reports for review.

Guava adds Visit Prep, medical-record summaries, lab results, doctor notes, documents, and supported patient-portal connections. It is the broader option when appointment preparation needs more than tracking entries.

If connected health data matters

CareClinic lists Apple Health and several fitness and wearable connections. Guava lists health apps, fitness devices, glucose monitors, and patient portals.

Connected data does not guarantee every metric, provider, record, or device will sync. Check the current supported-source list before moving an established health record.

If privacy and account setup matter

CareClinic's privacy policy explains its account, health-data, service-provider, export, and deletion practices. Its security information describes encryption for data in transit and at rest.

Guava uses an account to manage health information and connected records. Its privacy and security page says data is encrypted, users control sharing, and Guava does not sell health information.

Both apps can hold sensitive health information. Review current account deletion, export, backup, analytics, connected-service, and sharing rules before entering a full medication or medical history.

Where CareClinic fits better

  • You want medication, symptoms, measurements, appointments, and daily tasks in one care plan.
  • You need caregiver access, dependent profiles, or shared care routines.
  • You want Apple Watch support for a health-tracking routine.
  • You prefer a detailed daily diary and care report over connected patient portals.

Where Guava fits better

  • You want medication reminders, pill counts, and refill planning beside medical records.
  • You want to connect supported patient portals, lab results, doctor notes, or documents.
  • You need a web option as well as iPhone, iPad, and Android apps.
  • You want Visit Prep built around both tracking entries and health records.

When neither app may be enough

CareClinic and Guava are broad health trackers. If your main need is an ADHD-specific medication routine with quick taken or skipped records, estimated focus windows, reflections, refill planning, and medication notes, Pillr is a separate option to consider.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app supports every phone, tablet, watch, browser, portal, or connected device you plan to use.
  • Which history, report, caregiver, family, record, export, and insight features need a paid plan.
  • Whether medication reminders support your prescribed schedule and as-needed use.
  • How pill counts, refill alerts, missed entries, and postponed reminders work.
  • Which patient portals, health apps, and device sources are supported in your region.
  • How account deletion, export, backup, analytics, connected services, and health-data sharing work.

Is CareClinic or Guava better for medication tracking?

CareClinic may fit better if caregiver access, Apple Watch support, care plans, and a broad daily health diary matter. Guava may fit better if connected medical records, patient portals, pill counts, refill planning, and visit preparation matter.

Do CareClinic and Guava include medication reminders?

Yes. Both apps currently describe medication schedules, reminders, and medication records. Check the current plan and region details for the exact reminder, history, and refill tools you need.

Can CareClinic and Guava track symptoms with medication?

Yes. Both apps can keep symptom and medication records together. CareClinic centres this in a care plan and health diary, while Guava connects tracking with a broader personal health record.

Do CareClinic or Guava replace medical advice?

No. They are tracking and record-keeping tools. Questions about doses, timing, interactions, side effects, symptoms, and missed medication need advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
