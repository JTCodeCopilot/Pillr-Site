MyTherapy vs Dosecast
for medication tracking

MyTherapy and Dosecast both cover medication reminders and dose records. MyTherapy places medication beside symptoms, measurements, refills, appointments, and health reports, while Dosecast puts more emphasis on flexible interval schedules, postponing reminders, and reminder backup.

Published September 7, 2026 · Last reviewed September 7, 2026

A note from Pillr

This article is published by Pillr. We make a medication tracking app, but this page compares MyTherapy and Dosecast for people deciding between those two options.

Quick answer

Choose MyTherapy if you want medication reminders and refill tracking beside symptoms, mood, measurements, appointments, and a broader health diary.

Choose Dosecast if you want reminders based on the time since your last recorded dose, detailed schedule patterns, postponing, smart silencing, or a backup alert when an initial reminder goes unanswered.

Device availability may decide the choice before features do. MyTherapy currently has live listings for iPhone, iPad, and Android. Dosecast remains available on Google Play, but its Apple App Store listing was unavailable in the United States, Australia, and United Kingdom when checked on September 7, 2026.

Who this comparison is for

This comparison is for someone choosing between two medication reminder apps, especially when schedule flexibility, symptoms, refill tracking, reports, or device availability will decide the fit.

It is not a guide to choosing medication, setting a dose interval, changing a dose, or deciding what to do after a missed dose.

Key differences

MyTherapy is broader-health-diary led. Its current app overview places medication intake, refills, symptoms, mood, measurements, appointments, notes, and health reports inside one ongoing record.

Dosecast is reminder-control led. Its current feature page highlights offline reminders, backup alerts, postponing, smart silencing, varied schedule patterns, and reminders measured from the last recorded dose.

The overlap is enough for either app to support a medication routine. The practical difference is whether you want a wider health record around each dose or more control over when and how reminder alerts appear.

Detailed comparison by use case

If schedule flexibility is the main need

MyTherapy supports personalised medication reminders and a daily treatment plan. Its public information is strongest around regular reminders, intake records, refills, measurements, and appointments.

Dosecast describes daily, weekly, monthly, every-few-days, and interval-from-last-dose schedules. That last option can be useful when the reminder pattern depends on a recorded dose rather than a fixed clock time.

Schedule flexibility is not medical guidance. Only set a reminder pattern that reflects instructions from your prescriber or pharmacist.

If symptoms, mood, or measurements matter

MyTherapy treats symptom, pain, mood, weight, blood pressure, and other measurements as part of its main health diary. It also lists reminders for measurements and appointments.

Dosecast's current official information focuses more narrowly on medication reminders, dose history, refills, syncing, and multiple-person tracking. If a symptom or measurement record is essential, verify whether your current Dosecast edition can capture it in the form you need.

Neither app's charts or records prove that a medication caused or improved a symptom. Use them to organise questions for a healthcare appointment.

If missed reminders and late records matter

Dosecast can send a backup reminder if the first alert is not answered. It also describes postponing, smart silencing, skipping, and recording a dose later when you did not log it at the time.

MyTherapy records taken and skipped medication and builds an intake history. Check how each app shows the scheduled time, actual time, missed state, and later correction before depending on it for appointment preparation.

If reports, family use, or more than one device matter

MyTherapy can create health reports and a medication plan, and it describes one-time clinician sharing. Its wider diary may fit better when the report needs symptoms, mood, measurements, and medication together.

Dosecast Pro describes detailed medication history that can be viewed, emailed, or printed, plus tracking for multiple people and CloudSync across devices. Dosecast does not offer web access according to its current FAQ.

Family profiles, shared access, and tracking several people are different permission models. Confirm who can see, edit, and export each person's record before entering sensitive health information.

If privacy and account setup matter

MyTherapy's privacy policy says the app can be used without an account on one smartphone, but that setup cannot be restored. It also describes encrypted storage in a central European Union database, analytics, advertising consent, and extra consent for some partner features.

Dosecast says its Free edition does not require registration, while Pro CloudSync moves records between devices. Its privacy policy says it does not collect personally identifiable information during registration or operation but does describe aggregated usage data; Google Play separately lists data categories that may be collected. Review both sources together rather than assuming a reminder app is local-only.

Check current sign-in, deletion, export, analytics, backup, syncing, and family-sharing rules before entering a full medication history.

Where MyTherapy fits better

  • You want medication, symptoms, mood, measurements, appointments, and notes in one health diary.
  • You want refill and prescription reminders beside a broader treatment plan.
  • You want a health report that combines medication with wider health entries.
  • You need a currently listed iPhone, iPad, or Android app.

Where Dosecast fits better

  • You want reminders measured from the time of the last recorded dose.
  • You want backup alerts, postponing, or smart silencing.
  • You want detailed medication history, refill tracking, or multiple-person support and are comfortable checking the current Pro boundary.
  • You use Android and want an app whose reminders are designed to work without a data connection.

When neither app may be enough

MyTherapy and Dosecast are broad medication-management apps. If your main need is an ADHD-specific routine with quick taken or skipped records, estimated focus windows, reflections, refill planning, and medication notes, Pillr is a separate option to consider.

What to verify before choosing

  • Whether the app is currently available on your device and in your country.
  • Whether it supports the exact schedule your prescriber or pharmacist gave you.
  • How actual intake time, skipped entries, late records, corrections, and quantities appear in history.
  • Which refill, profile, syncing, report, export, and family tools need a paid option.
  • Whether a report contains the dates, medication details, symptoms, and measurements your appointment needs.
  • How sign-in, deletion, analytics, backup, sharing, and health-data handling work.

Is MyTherapy or Dosecast better for medication tracking?

MyTherapy may fit better if you want medication reminders beside symptoms, mood, measurements, refills, appointments, and health reports. Dosecast may fit better if flexible interval schedules, postponing reminders, backup alerts, and detailed dose history matter more.

Which app offers more flexible medication schedules?

Both support recurring medication reminders. Dosecast is more explicit about schedules based on the time since the last dose, plus daily, weekly, monthly, and multi-day patterns. Confirm that either app supports your exact prescribed routine before choosing.

Are MyTherapy and Dosecast available on iPhone and Android?

MyTherapy currently has live iPhone, iPad, and Android listings. Dosecast has a live Android listing, but its Apple App Store listing was unavailable in the United States, Australia, and United Kingdom when checked on September 7, 2026, despite the Dosecast website still describing iPhone support.

Do MyTherapy or Dosecast replace medical advice?

No. They are reminder and record-keeping tools. Questions about doses, timing, side effects, symptoms, and missed medication need advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
